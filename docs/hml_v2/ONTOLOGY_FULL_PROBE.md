# Whole-ontology probe — all 486 atoms

**Every atom in `MATHEMATICAL_REASONING_ONTOLOGY_V1` now has an explicit, executed
state.** Before this document, 78 of 486 atoms had been probed and 408 were recorded `?`
— never as absent, and never as present. This closes that hole.

The headline is deliberately unflattering: **162 of 486 atoms (33.3%) are PASS**, where
PASS requires the compiler to accept a faithful encoding *and* reject the corresponding
misuse. Acceptance alone was never scored as PASS, and 10 atoms that met the accept/reject
bar were explicitly demoted because a sub-capability the atom names is unreachable. A soft
number would have been worth nothing here.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 The five states, and how each is reached

The decision procedure is in `classify()` and runs identically for every atom.

| state | reached when |
|---|---|
| **PASS** | the faithful encoding compiles clean **and** the misuse probe is refused, **and** no downgrade applies |
| **PARTIAL** | the faithful encoding compiles clean but (a) the misuse also compiles clean, or (b) no misuse probe was constructible, or (c) a structural check came back false, or (d) an explicit downgrade applies |
| **BACKEND_ONLY** | the faithful encoding is refused, and the capability is reachable only by lowering the module to a named backend |
| **LOSSY** | the faithful spelling is refused, a weakened encoding compiles, that encoding is not vacuous, and the lost property is named |
| **ABSENT** | the faithful spelling is refused and either no fallback exists or the fallback is vacuous |
| **NOT_MEASURED** | no meaningful executable probe could be constructed; the reason is recorded per atom |

### 3.2 Three deliberate anti-inflation devices

An earlier revision of this file scored **189 PASS**. Auditing it found three ways the
number was soft. All three are now closed, and the corrected figure is 162.

**(a) Rejection-for-the-wrong-reason.** 49 PASS verdicts rested on a parse or
name-resolution error. Many were legitimate — an unbound free variable rejected with
`HML-N301` *is* the enforcement `LOGIC.FREE_VS_BOUND` asks for. But roughly half were
probes of invented syntax for an absent feature: `require x >= 0.0 for i in 1 to 10`
fails with `HML-P204` because there is no scoped-constraint syntax, which is evidence of
absence, not of enforcement. 22 probes were replaced with `reject=None`, which by
construction can no longer yield PASS.

**(b) No structural evidence path.** Ten atoms — alpha-equivalence and alpha-renaming,
canonicalisation, uniform continuity, round-trip fidelity, declared translation loss,
loss annotation, backend compilation, multiple views, and querying statements — have no
source-level misuse to reject at all. Rather than give them PASS for free or punish them for the shape of the question,
they take a `structural=(check, key)` reference to a boolean produced by one of four
executed check functions. `XFORM.ROUND_TRIP` is PASS because six modules were emitted,
re-parsed and compared by content hash and all six matched — not because it seemed
plausible.

**(c) Vacuous fallbacks laundering ABSENT into LOSSY.** `var A : Matrix[Real,3,3]` plus
`var t : Real` plus `require t = t` is not an encoding of a trace; it is an unrelated
scalar sitting next to a matrix. `_is_vacuous()` applies a mechanical test — the stand-in
symbol (the last declaration) must carry a structured type, or some proposition must be
more than a syntactic self-identity — and reclassifies the failures to ABSENT. This moved
18 atoms out of LOSSY, including `ALG.DETERMINANT`, `ALG.TRACE`, `ALG.RANK`,
`ANA.GRADIENT`, `ANA.HESSIAN` and `NUMER.FLOATING_POINT`.

### 3.3 Explicit downgrades

Ten atoms cleared their evidence bar and were still demoted to PARTIAL, because the atom
names a sub-capability that is unreachable. Each downgrade states what is missing. (An
eleventh, `ANA.PARTIAL_FUNCTION`, carries the same kind of caveat but reaches PARTIAL by
the ordinary route — its misuse probe compiles clean; see §4.)

|---|---|---|---|
| `LOGIC.VARIABLE_BINDING` | CRIT | scope ends are enforced (`HML-N301` after the binder closes) | the atom asks that shadowing be *resolvable*; HML rejects it outright |
| `LOGIC.PROOF_OBLIGATION` | HIGH | an undischarged obligation blocks `PROVED` (`HML-R710`) | the `obligation` statement is unimplemented, so obligations cannot be declared or discharged by hand |
| `ANA.CONTINUITY` | CRIT | the domain argument is mandatory | continuity *at a point* has no spelling (`HML-P266`) |
| `ANA.DERIVATIVE` | CRIT | the binder must be typed | no evaluation-at form: `f'(a)` and `f'` are the same expression |
| `ANA.INTEGRAL_DEFINITE` | CRIT | binder and bounds are mandatory | no measure or differential |
| `ANA.LIMIT` | CRIT | the binder must be typed | no one-sided limits; `limit(…)` denotes a value, not a quantified statement |

### 3.4 Judgement calls

**103 atoms carry an explicit `judgement` note** saying why the chosen encoding is
arguable. They are in the JSON under the `judgement` key and reproduced in the table
below. Two are worth flagging because they disagree with prior work:

* **`LOGIC.INDEXED_FAMILY` (CRITICAL).** The original audit scored this `P` on the
  strength of `index i : Natural` compiling. It compiles, but `index` declares a bare
  symbol that no aggregate, law or constraint can range over, and there is no subscript
  form (`X[i]` is `HML-P208`). The probe here uses the only real encoding of a family —
  `Function[Natural -> T]`, which does compile — and scores **PARTIAL**, because no
  misuse of a family can be constructed when nothing treats it as one. The disagreement
  with the published `P` is recorded in the atom's own note.
* **`DISC.DAG`.** Acyclicity genuinely *is* checked in HML — on the definition graph and
  on the reasoning graph — but not on a user-declared graph value, because a graph is
  only a set of pairs. The two are different objects. Scored on what the probe shows and
  flagged.

### 3.5 What the probes could not do

The five-state vocabulary treats "the compiler rejects the misuse" as enforcement. It
cannot distinguish *rejecting for the right reason* from *rejecting for a coincidental
one* beyond what the diagnostic code shows, and every PASS verdict's code is printed in
the table so a reader can check. It also cannot measure whether an accepted encoding
means what a mathematician would take it to mean; that is what the `judgement` notes are
for.

Six of these deserve naming because they are cheap to close and currently make the type
system look stronger than it is:

* **`A + B` on non-conformable matrices compiles clean.** Vector addition *is* shape
  checked (`HML-T433`) and matrix multiplication *is* conformability checked
  (`HML-T434`); matrix addition and subtraction are not checked at all. This is almost
  certainly an oversight rather than a design choice, and it is the single most
  surprising result in the run.
* **`require p < q` on two `Proposition`s compiles clean**, as does `p > q`. Ordering is
  available on a type that has no order.
* **`require f = g` where `f : Function[Real -> NonNegativeReal]` and
  `g : Function[Real -> Real]` compiles clean**, so the codomain is not part of function
  identity in the way `ANA.DOMAIN_CODOMAIN` requires.
* **`obs y : Real` with no value compiles clean.** An observation that observes nothing
  is exactly the population/sample distinction collapsing.
* **`minimise f` followed by `maximise f` on the same objective compiles clean.** Two
  contradictory senses coexist silently.
* **A function-typed variable applied outside its declared domain is unchecked.**
  `var f : Function[PositiveReal -> Real]` then `f(x)` with `x : Real` compiles clean,
  while the same thing through a `def` is refused (`HML-T472`). The refinement is
  enforced on definitions and not on function-typed values.

Two further behaviours are worth recording as design questions rather than defects:
`prior mu ~ …` on a plain `var` is accepted (a prior over something that is not a
parameter), and `minimise` over an `obs`-declared symbol is accepted (optimising over
data).

## 5. NOT_MEASURED — 24 atoms, and why

`NOT_MEASURED` is an honest answer and was preferred to a guess wherever a probe would
have amounted to inventing a syntax and then reporting that the invention did not parse.
That measures the inventor, not the language.

The distribution is reassuring: **22 of the 24 are LOW criticality**, one is MEDIUM
(`ANA.INITIAL_CONDITION`), one is MEDIUM (`XFORM.STAGING`), and **none is CRITICAL or

manifold for a chart to live on, no category for a functor to act on, no string type for

## 6. CANDIDATE GAPS — for §13's second half, **not** added here

**No atom was added, and none should be added on the strength of this section alone.**
Adding atoms is the second half of directive §13 and is gated on this measurement being
finished. What follows is the evidence that half will need: which mathematical families
the *existing* ontology already covers and HML does not, ranked by how badly.

A family appearing here does **not** mean the ontology is missing atoms for it. In most
cases the ontology covers the family perfectly well and HML does not implement it — that
is a language gap, not an ontology gap. The two are separated below.

### 6.2 The three structural causes, and what they would cost

Almost every zero above traces to one of three missing pieces. They are worth naming
because closing one closes dozens of atoms at once, and because a candidate atom that
does not trace to one of them is probably a genuine ontology gap rather than a language
gap.

**Cause 1 — there is no indexed binder over a range.** `sum` and `prod` take exactly one
aggregate argument (`HML-T426`); there is no `sum(a(i), i : Natural, 1, 10)`. This alone
accounts for `DISC.SUMMATION` (CRITICAL), `DISC.PRODUCT_NOTATION`, `ANA.SERIES`,
`ANA.POWER_SERIES`, `DISC.GENERATING_FUNCTION`, `PROB.IID`, `STAT.TIME_SERIES`,
`LOGIC.INDEXED_UNION_INTERSECTION`, `DISC.FLOW` and `OPT.CONSTRAINT_SCOPE` — ten atoms,
one of them CRITICAL. There is also no subscript form, so `X[i]` is `HML-P208` and an
"indexed family" is only ever a single symbol beside an unused `index` declaration.

**Cause 2 — a binder may never reuse a declared name.** `derivative(f(x), x : Real)` when
`x` is already declared is `HML-N302`. The consequences are sharp and, I think,
unintended:

* `d²f/dx²` has no spelling — repeated differentiation in the *same* variable is
  rejected, and only a mixed second derivative in two invented names compiles
  (`ANA.HIGHER_DERIVATIVE`).
* An ODE `y'(t) = y(t)` cannot be written with one `t`; the two sides must use different
  symbols and nothing records that they are the same point (`ANA.ODE`, `ANA.PDE`).
* The gradient of an objective **cannot be taken with respect to the decision variable**
  — the binder shadows the `param` — so `OPT.GRADIENT_OF_OBJECTIVE` and `OPT.KKT` are
  both LOSSY for the same reason.

This is a scoping rule interacting badly with the D-026 binding expressions. It is a
language defect worth a decision record, not an ontology gap.

**Cause 3 — there are no unit-bearing literals and no unit objects.** `const g :
Quantity[Length / Time^2] = 9.81` is `HML-T402`, so a physical constant must be declared
dimensionless — losing exactly the unit the atom requires (`SCI.PHYSICAL_CONSTANT`,
ABSENT). Dimensions exist and are excellent; units do not exist at all, which makes
`SCI.UNIT`, `SCI.UNIT_CONVERSION`, `SCI.PREFIX_SCALING`, `SCI.AFFINE_UNIT` and
`SCI.TIME_AND_CALENDAR` unreachable by design. Note also two asymmetries found by probe:
`abs` refuses a `Quantity` (`HML-T427`), so a dimensionful absolute error cannot be
written; and `log` refuses a dimensionless `Quantity` while `exp` accepts the same
expression.

## 7. Relationship to the published "76.9% faithful over probed atoms"

**This measurement does not contradict that figure, and it does not supersede it in kind.
It measures a different thing over a different denominator with a different vocabulary.**

`docs/hml_v2/AUDIT_RERUN.md` §2 reports 76.9% "faithful of all probed atoms (78)". That
document is explicit and correct about its own scope: §3.3 says "These are percentages
over 78 probed atoms out of 486. **They are not whole-ontology coverage and must not be
quoted as such**", and the ledger row records "408 of 486 ontology atoms — `NOT_MEASURED`.
Every percentage here is over probed atoms only." Nothing in that document claimed a
whole-ontology number.

Three differences make the figures non-comparable, and the 33.3% here must **not** be read
as a fall from 76.9%:

1. **Denominator.** 78 probed atoms then; 486 now, of which 462 carry a verdict. The 78
   were the CRITICAL set plus the composition specimens — that is, selected for being the
   language's designed strengths. The other 408 were never a random sample of anything;
   they are the remainder, and the remainder is harder.
2. **Vocabulary.** "Faithful" there means `P` — the probe compiled clean. Here, compiling
   clean is at most PARTIAL. PASS additionally requires the misuse to be rejected. Had
   this document used the audit's `P` criterion, the whole-ontology figure would be
   **233 of 486 (47.9%)** — PASS plus PARTIAL, both of which compile a faithful encoding.
   That is the honest apples-to-apples restatement.
3. **Direction of effort.** The audit's probes were written to be fair to HML's idiom
   (see the 17 idiom corrections). These were written to be fair *and* adversarial: every
   atom that could have a misuse probe got one.

On the audit's own denominator and its own criterion, this run reproduces it. Of the 64
CRITICAL atoms, 47 PASS + 15 PARTIAL = **62 compile a faithful encoding, 96.9%**, which
is exactly the `AFTER-c` critical figure the rerun published. The two exceptions are
LOSSY, and they are precisely `LOGIC.PREDICATE` and `DISC.SUMMATION` — the same two that
`AUDIT_RERUN.md` §3.2 reports as `L` in every column. The independent reproduction is
therefore exact, atom for atom. The one verdict that changes character is
`LOGIC.INDEXED_FAMILY`, argued in §3.4; it is PARTIAL/LOSSY here rather than `P`, which
would move the critical faithful figure to 61 of 64 (95.3%) under this document's
stricter reading.

**What this document adds** is not a better number; it is the removal of an unknown. The
408 atoms that were `?` are now 21 ABSENT, 24 NOT_MEASURED, and 363 with an executed
state. Any future claim about HML's mathematical coverage now has a denominator that is
the whole ontology, and a PASS criterion that acceptance alone cannot satisfy.
